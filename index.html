How the scope expanded

I did not leave communications behind. Each role gave me a closer view of the product, the market and the business decisions around it.

  1. 2016Marketing and response

    At AIESEC, I led a 12-person team and helped increase leads by 83%. It taught me early that communication had to be tied to a specific response.

  2. 2018 to 2020Context and consequence

    Public affairs, fashion and social impact put me in very different rooms. A statement, a launch or a CSR programme could sound completely different depending on who was hearing it—and what they had at stake.

  3. 2020 to 2023Regional relevance

    At Zendesk, I worked across six Latin American markets and the Caribbean. At WeWork, I moved inside the company and learned that employees should never be the last people to understand what is happening.

  4. 2023 to 2026Product and commercial depth

    At 100 Ladrillos, ThinkY and Belvo, the remit expanded into positioning, launches, founder and executive communications, events, CRM and measurement.

  5. NowThe work I do now

    Today I work across those disciplines when the problem requires them. The point is not breadth for its own sake; it is being able to make better decisions across the whole programme.

How I approach the work

Most of my work begins before the channel is chosen.

The first question is rarely “What should we publish?” It is usually “What needs to change, for whom, and what is getting in the way?”

From there, I can move from research and positioning into Executive Communications, PR, launches, events, CRM or Sales Enablement.

Only then do I choose the channel.

That gives each channel a clear job inside the programme.

When the category is complexMake the decision easier to understand.

Customer insight · positioning · messaging · GTM launches · SEO content

When trust is fragilePut credible proof where people can see it.

When markets differKeep the strategy; change the evidence and language.
