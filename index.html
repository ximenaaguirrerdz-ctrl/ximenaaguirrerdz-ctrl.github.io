Ten years in five chapters

Each role added the part the last one was missing: attention, context, trust, systems and commercial follow-through.

  1. 2016Attention was only useful if something happened next.

    At AIESEC, I led twelve people and helped increase leads by 83%. It was the first time I saw the difference between getting noticed and getting someone to act.

  2. 2018 to 2020Context changed everything.

    Public affairs, fashion and social impact put me in very different rooms. A statement, a launch or a CSR programme could sound completely different depending on who was hearing it—and what they had at stake.

  3. 2020 to 2023One story could not sound the same everywhere.

    At Zendesk, I worked across six Latin American markets and the Caribbean. At WeWork, I moved inside the company and learned that employees should never be the last people to understand what is happening.

  4. 2023 to 2026I got closer to the product, Sales and the numbers.

    At 100 Ladrillos, ThinkY and Belvo, a story became a launch, a founder interview, an event, a CRM follow-up and eventually something I could measure.

  5. NowI don’t see Communications, Product Marketing, PR and Field as separate boxes.

    I think about what needs to make sense, who needs to believe it and what should happen next.

How the work actually happens

The work rarely stays in one lane. Neither do I.

A launch can become a founder interview, an internal FAQ, a customer story, a field event and a Sales follow-up in the same week. That’s the part I like.

I get close enough to the product to understand what is true, to the audience to know what matters and to execution to see whether the idea survived contact with the real world.

Product → message → people → market → follow-up.

Not because every channel needs a campaign. Because the pieces usually affect each other.

Applied AI + content operations

The model could already write. Context was the hard part.

The problem was making sure it knew what was true, what sounded like us, what changed by market and where a human needed to say no.

At Belvo, I worked with engineering to create Content Cosmos, an internal AI-enabled editorial workflow grounded in approved product knowledge, brand voice, market context and editorial standards.

I structured the working context and kept source review, factual approval, cultural judgment and the final voice with humans. Faster first drafts. Same human accountability.
